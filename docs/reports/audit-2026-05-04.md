  - `ce074172 Per-encoder CA params + SimLex sweep`

These are climbing native-encoder numbers, not MiniLM-dominance numbers.

### Proposed rewrite

> ## 1.2 The encoder layer is plural and contested `[ACTIVE RESEARCH]`
>
> Wheeler runs multiple encoder backends and treats their relative SimLex performance as the live signal of architectural progress:
>
> - **`hippocampus`** — Wheeler-native n-gram encoder. **Default** for SimLex evaluation and the active production target.
> - **`embedding`** (MiniLM, `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) — Strong external baseline used for comparison.

>

>
> **Wheeler still handles memory** — reconstruction via attractor dynamics on the three grids. The CA is not a similarity function. That part of the division is correct.

## §3 The three grids — **Missing/Terminology** (Cortex layer)

### What's missing

Canon describes three grids (Corpus, Experiential, SCM) and the recall formula. It does **not** mention the Cortex 3-tier semantic scoring system, which is a major architectural component:

- `wheeler_memory/cortex.py` — top-level cortex orchestration
- `wheeler_memory/cortex_scm.py` — Structural Coherence Measure (different from §3.3 SCM grid)
- `wheeler_memory/cortex_classifier.py` — L3 native classifier
- `tests/test_cortex.py`, `tests/test_cortex_classifier.py`, `tests/test_cortex_scm.py`

Per the existing `wheeler-memory/CLAUDE.md`:

> **Cortex System (3-tier semantic scoring)**
> - **L1**: Pearson correlation adjacency graph over retrieved attractors, BFS clustering
> - **L2**: Settlement CA — opinion diffusion on the correlation graph until convergence
> - **L3**: Native semantic classifier — scores choices without external models

### Proposed addition (new §3.5)

> ## 3.5 Cortex — three-tier semantic scoring `[BUILT]`
>
> A scoring layer over retrieved attractors, distinct from the three grids. Three tiers:
>
> 1. **L1 — Correlation graph.** Pearson correlation adjacency over the retrieved attractor set, BFS clustering to identify coherent neighborhoods.
> 2. **L2 — Settlement CA.** Opinion diffusion on the correlation graph until convergence. This *is* a CA, but on the abstract graph topology, not the grid.
> 3. **L3 — Native classifier.** `cortex_classifier.py` scores choices without external models. Shipped via `train_cortex_classifier.py`.
>
> The classification output (`cortex_scm.py:228 SCMResult`) is one of `SYNTHESIS / NOVEL / HALLUCINATION`, with ten layer scores and a net warrant. **This SCM is a Measure, not a Map** — the terminology collides with §3.3 but the objects are different.

## §3 Corpus grid framing — **Terminology**

### Current canon

> | Corpus | Cold | Slow / batch | Stable knowledge layer |

Implies a single global "Corpus grid."

### Evidence

There is no `corpus.py` or `corpus_grid.py`. "Corpus" in code refers to **per-basin attractors** stored in chunked storage (`storage.py`, `chunking.py`, `brick.py`). Each stored memory is a corpus attractor; there is no monolithic 64×64 corpus grid.

The interference function (`interference.py:58–84`) takes a `corpus_att` argument — that's a single retrieved attractor, not a global corpus state.

### Proposed clarification

Add a footnote to the §3 grid table:

> **Note:** "Corpus" is not a single global 64×64 grid like Experiential and SCM. The Corpus layer is the *set* of stored basin attractors held in chunked storage (`storage.py`, `chunking.py`); the recall formula operates on individual retrieved corpus attractors paired against the live Experiential grid.

- `tests/test_ternary.py` exists — ternary is real, but applied as a discretization layer.

### Proposed clarification

> ## 2. State space — continuous frames with ternary snapping
>
> Frames are `float32` over `[-1, +1]`. Ternary `{-1, 0, +1}` is the **discretization alphabet** applied via `TERNARY_SNAP_MODE` (topological or threshold-based, with thresholds at ±0.33). Zero remains the reconstruction root in both the continuous and discretized views.
>
> Independently derived; later mapped to Setun (1958) and BitNet b1.58 (2024).
